
 ABSTRACT
Hospital Emergency Room Triage is a critical process used to assess and prioritize patients according to the severity and urgency of their medical conditions. In a busy emergency department, patients may arrive with a wide range of problems, from minor injuries and illnesses to life-threatening conditions requiring immediate intervention. Triage helps healthcare professionals identify those who need urgent treatment and ensures that available medical resources are used effectively.
The triage process begins when a patient arrives at the emergency room. A healthcare professional collects essential information about the patient's symptoms, medical history, vital signs, and current condition. Based on this initial assessment, patients are categorized according to the level of urgency. Patients with severe or life-threatening conditions are given immediate attention, while patients with less serious conditions may safely wait until the emergency team is available. This prioritization helps reduce delays in treating critically ill patients and supports the efficient functioning of the emergency department.
Effective triage requires trained healthcare professionals, clear communication, accurate assessment, and appropriate decision-making. Modern emergency departments may also use standardized triage systems and digital technologies to support patient assessment, documentation, monitoring, and resource allocation. Continuous reassessment is important because a patient's condition can change while waiting for treatment.
Hospital Emergency Room Triage therefore plays an essential role in improving patient safety, reducing treatment delays, managing emergency department workload, and organizing the flow of patients. A well-structured triage system allows healthcare teams to respond appropriately to emergencies while maintaining efficient patient care. Understanding the principles and importance of triage is essential for healthcare professionals because timely prioritization can significantly influence the quality and effectiveness of emergency medical services.
